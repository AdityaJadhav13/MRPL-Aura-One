# Dossier V0 — optical validation of the phone reader

**Status:** protocol written, **no data collected**
**Needs:** a printer, a ruler, several phones, and a few hours
**Does NOT need:** H₂S, a laboratory, any chemistry, or any of gates S1–S3

---

## 1. What this experiment is, and what it is not

> **V0 is an experiment about the PHONE READER. It is not an H₂S experiment.**

It photographs printed optical targets. There is no chemistry, no gas, no dose.
Nothing it produces is evidence about H₂S, and no result from it may be
described in terms of exposure.

What it *is*: the first physical evidence that the software half of the
instrument works. Every threshold in the pipeline is currently a **provisional**
value chosen to be defensible on rendered fixtures. V0 is how they stop being
guesses. It is also the only substantial validation available while gates S1,
S2 and S3 remain open, which makes it the most valuable thing the software side
can do right now.

## 2. Materials

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target** | `measurement-engine/badge-print/badge-v1-target.svg`, printed **at 100% scale**. Generated by `dart run tool/generate_printable_target.dart`. |
| **Scale check** | The target carries a printed 20 mm bar. **Measure it with a ruler before photographing anything.** "Fit to page" silently rescales, and every millimetre-space conclusion would then be wrong by that factor. |
| **Substrates** | At minimum matte paper. Preferably also a semi-gloss stock, because gloss is what produces specular highlights and the glare-rejection thresholds need a realistic stimulus. |
| **Mounting** | Flat, on a rigid board, for the baseline condition. |
| **Curved mount** | One target on a cylinder of roughly wrist radius (≈ 25 mm). This is the only way to set a bend tolerance. |
| **Phones** | As many as can be borrowed. See §4. |

Print the target **several times**, including at least one deliberately poor
print (draft mode, low ink, or a photocopy of a print) for the degradation
condition.

## 3. Ground truth — the part most likely to be got wrong

> Do not trust printer RGB, screen RGB, or the design file.

The values in `badgeV1Colours` are **design-space** values. What lands on paper
is not what the design file said: ink, substrate, printer profile and dithering
all intervene. Treating design values as physical colour ground truth would
mean the colour correction is fitted toward colours the patches never had, and
that error would be baked into every measurement afterwards.

**Preferred:** measure the printed patches with a spectrophotometer or
colorimeter. Record CIELAB under a stated illuminant and observer. These go
into `reference_profiles.patch_values` as **measured** values.

**If no instrument is available:** proceed, but record design-space values
explicitly labelled `provisional: design-space, not measured`, and treat every
absolute colour conclusion as unavailable. Relative conclusions — does
correction reduce spread across phones? — remain valid, because they compare
photographs to each other rather than to a standard.

**Record for every print:** printer make and model, driver settings, paper
stock, ink or toner, print date, target file version, and the measured length
of the 20 mm scale bar.

## 4. The condition matrix

Do **not** manufacture good numbers by photographing one perfect setup
repeatedly. The matrix exists to be hostile.

### Phones — at least 4, spanning the range

| Slot | Aim |
|---|---|
| 1 | A recent flagship |
| 2 | A mid-range Android, ideally 2–4 years old |
| 3 | **A low-end Android** — the device a refinery contractor actually carries |
| 4 | An iPhone |

Record the `CameraCapabilities` the app reports for each. **A phone that lacks
manual white balance is not excluded**: whether that control is needed is
precisely question Q9, and the printed references exist to make the illuminant
recoverable without it.

### Illuminants — at least 5

Daylight near a window · direct sunlight · warm indoor LED (~3000 K) · cool
LED (~5000 K) · fluorescent if available · **mixed** (window plus room light,
the realistic worst case, and the one no two-point correction can handle).

Record an approximate colour temperature and whether the source was direct or
diffuse.

### Geometry — at least 4 per phone/illuminant cell

Near-normal (phone parallel, badge centred) · moderate perspective (roughly
20–30° tilt) · rotated (roughly 45°, because that is where the fill-ratio floor
bites — see `research/fiducial-benchmark.md` §4.2) · near and far distance
extremes.

### Deliberate failures — the most valuable images in the set

These establish that refusal works, which matters more than that acceptance
works.

| Condition | What it should establish |
|---|---|
| Motion blur | The sharpness floor |
| Specular glare on the badge | The glare and clipping limits |
| Partial shadow across the badge | Whether adaptive thresholding copes |
| Clipped highlight (strong direct light) | That a clipped reference is refused, not corrected |
| One corner marker covered | **That the pose is refused, not invented.** Benchmark finding M0B-5. |
| A secondary marker covered | That partial corruption is localised, not global |
| Curved / bent target | The bend tolerance — the whole point of the redundant control points |
| The degraded print | Print-quality limits |
| Badge on a dark surface, and on a light one | The quiet-zone rim interaction |

## 5. Procedure

1. Print, then **measure the 20 mm bar**. Record it. If it is not 20 mm, fix
   the print settings and start again.
2. For each phone: open the capture screen, record the reported
   `CameraCapabilities` and the applied-versus-requested settings.
3. For each illuminant × geometry cell: use the live guidance, capture when it
   permits, and **also** force a manual capture in a cell the guidance refuses.
   The refused ones are data.
4. Keep the **original bytes** of every still. Features can be recomputed; a
   discarded original cannot.
5. Record, per image: phone, illuminant, geometry, substrate, print instance,
   and any deliberate failure applied.
6. Store under `data/lab/` with `data_origin: LAB`. These are laboratory
   images — controlled and traceable — not simulated and not field.

## 6. The questions, and how each is answered

| | Question | Answered by |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | How reliably can we detect the badge? | Detection rate over the whole matrix, per phone and per condition. Re-run `tool/fiducial_benchmark.dart`'s metrics on photographs. |
| **Q2** | How accurately can we rectify geometry? | Reprojection of the **secondary** markers, in millimetres. Not the primaries — their residual is zero by construction. |
| **Q3** | Can redundant points identify non-planarity better than the four fitting points? | Flat versus curved targets. The four-point residual should be ~0 for both; the withheld-marker residual should separate them. **If it does not, the redundant-control design has failed and must be redesigned.** |
| **Q4** | How much raw colour variation occurs across phones? | Spread of raw patch CIELAB for the same printed target, same illuminant, across phones. The baseline everything else is measured against. |
| **Q5** | How much across illuminants? | Same, holding phone fixed and varying illuminant. |
| **Q6** | How much does QRsens B/W correction reduce that? | Apply `BlackWhiteForm.qrsensEquation2` using the black and light-neutral patches; recompute Q4/Q5 spread. |
| **Q7** | How much does multi-reference affine/CCM correction reduce it? | Fit on six patches, recompute spread. Compare against Q6 on identical images. |
| **Q8** | Does correction generalise to withheld colours? | ΔE₀₀ on the four held-out patches. **This is the number that sets `reference_profiles.max_delta_e`,** currently a provisional 2.0. |
| **Q9** | Which camera capabilities materially affect performance? | Group Q4–Q8 results by reported `CameraCapabilities`. Specifically: does a phone without exposure/focus lock do measurably worse? |
| **Q10** | Which failure modes can we reliably detect and refuse? | The deliberate-failure set. Report detection rate **and false-accept rate** per mode. |
| **Q11** | Is controlled-light capture necessary? | If Q8 residuals stay within tolerance across all illuminants including mixed, ambient capture suffices. If they do not, it does not. |
| **Q12** | Would a simple hood or accessory materially help? | Only if Q11 says no. Compare a subset shot through a simple opaque hood against the same cells shot ambient. |

**Do not prejudge the answers.** In particular, Q11 has a plausible outcome —
mixed illumination defeats any per-patch correction — that would change the
product, and the experiment should be run in a way that can produce it.

## 7. Thresholds this experiment is meant to set

Every one is currently provisional and labelled as such in code.

| Threshold | Where | Currently |
|---|---|---|
| Colour-correction hold-out limit | `validateCorrection(maximumDeltaE00:)` | 2.0, provisional |
| Sharpness floor | `AcquisitionLimits.minimumLaplacianVariance` | 1e-4, provisional, and scale-dependent |
| Clipping and glare limits | `AcquisitionLimits` | 0.02 / 0.01, provisional |
| Exposure window | `AcquisitionLimits` luma mean | 0.18–0.92, provisional |
| Scale window | `AcquisitionLimits` px/mm | 8–40, provisional |
| Tilt limit | `AcquisitionLimits.maximumTiltRatio` | 1.25, provisional |
| Auto-capture run and drift | `AutoCaptureGate` | 5 frames / 600 ms / 6 px, provisional |
| Blob shape limits | `BlobFilter` | provisional; fill ratio must stay **below 0.5** |
| **Bend tolerance** | not yet implemented | **no value exists** — `validateGeometry` measures and does not judge |
| Preview-versus-still divergence | not yet characterised | §8 |

## 8. Preview versus still — record the difference

Directive §11. The preview stream and the captured still may differ in
resolution, crop, exposure and colour pipeline, and the app already re-runs the
authoritative checks on the still for that reason.

V0 should **quantify** the gap: for each capture, record the last preview
assessment alongside the still assessment, and report how often a preview that
said `ready` produced a still that was refused, and why. If that rate is high,
guidance thresholds need tightening relative to the still thresholds so workers
are not repeatedly told to retake a photo the app just told them to take.

## 9. Output

1. `research/dossier-v0-results.md` — the answers to Q1–Q12, with the numbers.
2. Updated thresholds in code, each with the evidence cited and the
   `provisional` flag cleared only where it is genuinely earned.
3. A re-run of `research/fiducial-benchmark.md` §3 on photographs, plus the
   ArUco and AprilTag comparison that could not be run on synthetic data.
4. `reference_profiles` rows carrying **measured** patch values, if an
   instrument was available.

## 10. What V0 cannot tell us

Stated so the results are not over-read.

- Nothing about H₂S, chemistry, dose, or any gate S1–S3 question.
- Nothing about the real badge's substrate: a printed paper target is not a
  chemically coated cartridge, and its optical behaviour will differ.
- Nothing about ageing, storage or shelf life.
- Nothing about how a badge looks after eight hours on a wrist.

It tells us whether a phone can read a printed target accurately enough to be
worth pointing at a real one. That is a genuine and currently unanswered
question, and it is the right one to answer next.
